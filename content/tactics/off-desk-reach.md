## Why it works

At work, buyers are surrounded by vendors trying to sell to them, and their guard is up. At home or on holiday, scrolling Instagram or watching TV, they are not in evaluation mode. A light, entertaining ad in that context feels less like a sales attempt and more like content.

That relaxed context also means the ad is more likely to leave a good feeling than an irritated one. The feeling sticks to the brand and colours later judgement.

And while everyone else pauses campaigns for summer, you keep building familiarity, so you are top of mind when they return.

## Steps

1. **Identify when your buyers are away.** For example, CFOs in July and August, or the end-of-year holidays.
2. **Choose off-desk channels:** Meta (Facebook and Instagram), connected TV and other places people spend leisure time.
3. **Target your CRM account lists only.** Never rely on interest targeting like "interested in computer software".
4. **Use creative that fits the context:** human, vertical video, light or funny. Not a feature list.
5. **Keep frequency moderate.** The aim is to stay top of mind without being pushy.

## Example

CFOs take time off in July and August, and Tabs did not want to stop reaching them. They added CTV and Meta, where people spend time when they are not at their desk, which let them stay top of mind without being pushy. Their claw-machine brand video runs in vertical format on Meta.

## Prerequisites

- A target account list that can be matched to Meta and CTV audiences.
- Creative built for the format (vertical, short, human).

## How to measure

- Reach within target accounts during the period.
- Website visit lift from target accounts compared with a holdout group.
- Pipeline from those accounts in the months after.

## Pitfalls

- Running the same corporate creative you use on LinkedIn.
- Broad interest targeting, which reaches people with no buying power.
- Judging it on immediate leads. This is about staying remembered.
