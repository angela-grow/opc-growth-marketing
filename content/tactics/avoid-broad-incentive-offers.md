## Why it backfires

It works for the wrong goal. Incentives reliably increase the number of demo requests, which is why the tactic is tempting. But the people most attracted to a free gift are the people who want the gift. Your real buyers, senior people with an actual problem, are the least motivated by a pair of headphones.

The reward also changes why people take the meeting. Someone who books a demo to get a gift card shows up for the gift card. There is no genuine interest to build on.

The result is a pipeline that looks healthy on a dashboard and then wastes sales time on calls that go nowhere.

## What to do instead

1. **Target your CRM account lists, not interest audiences.** Make sure only people at companies you want can see the offer.
2. **If you use incentives, offer them only to that tight audience.** Tabs found incentives worked well once the audience was their own account lists.
3. **Replace the gift with value that only buyers care about.** A benchmark report, a teardown of their current setup, or a useful tool.
4. **Judge offers on pipeline quality,** not on the number of form fills.

## Example

When Tabs started on Meta, they used native targeting with affinity audiences and promoted incentives like AirPods for taking a demo. They got a lot of junk: non-ICP companies and bad persona fits. When they switched exclusively to CRM account lists, lead quality improved massively, both for webinar promotion and for demo requests with incentives.

## How to measure

If you suspect this is happening, compare the share of leads from incentive offers that match your ICP, and their conversion to qualified opportunities, against other sources.

## Pitfalls

- Celebrating a falling cost per lead without checking who the leads are.
- Assuming "the incentive doesn't work" when the real problem was the audience.
