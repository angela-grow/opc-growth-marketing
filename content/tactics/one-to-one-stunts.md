## Why it works

Nothing gets attention like your own name. A message made for one person is almost impossible to ignore, and far more memorable than anything sent to many.

The effort itself is the message. When someone sees that a company went to real trouble just for them, they feel a pull to respond, even if only to acknowledge it. That is the opening a sales team needs.

Done publicly and with a sense of humour, these stunts also tend to get shared, so the target's network sees it too. One stunt can become free reach.

## Steps

1. **Limit it to your most valuable, most likely accounts.** Only accounts in active focus, where you know the context and the people.
2. **Research the person.** Their role, what they have said publicly, what they care about.
3. **Connect the gesture to their context.** The best one-to-one ads talk about the account's situation as the solution, not just their name.
4. **Pick the format.** A billboard near their office, a personalised microsite, a LinkedIn ad with their company's use case, or a thoughtful gift.
5. **Keep it playful and short.** One line they will smile at.
6. **Have sales ready to follow up** the moment the person responds.

## Example

Sentry ran one purple billboard naming John Deere's CTO, Jahmy Hindman: "Jahmy, you told us this would work." He commented on LinkedIn: "Ha! Traveling out of the country for a few weeks but it still got my attention! I'd say you did it right!" Tabs is also implementing Userled, which creates one-to-one LinkedIn ads ("[Company], you have complex billing, find out how we solve it") plus a microsite for each company.

## Prerequisites

- An account where a single deal justifies the cost.
- Real knowledge of the person and their situation.
- Sales ready to act on the response.

## How to measure

- Response from the target (reply, comment, meeting).
- Opportunity created or deal progressed within the following weeks.
- Treat each stunt as a bet. You only find out what works by testing.

## Pitfalls

- Running it for accounts unlikely to convert. Every one-to-one tactic is a bet, so place bets on accounts close to pipeline.
- Crossing the line into creepy by using personal information they did not share publicly.
- No follow-up plan, so the moment of attention is wasted.
