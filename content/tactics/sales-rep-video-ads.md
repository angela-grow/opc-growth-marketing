## Why it works

By the time a prospect gets on a call, they have already seen the rep on their feed several times. The rep is no longer a stranger. Familiarity with a face builds liking, and people say yes more easily to people they like.

The rep also shows up as an expert, talking about the buyer's problems in plain language. That positions them as someone worth listening to, so they join the first call from a position of authority rather than as a salesperson asking for time.

The effect reaches people the rep has never met. A CFO who has seen the videos may "know of" the company and negotiate less hard because of that familiarity.

There is an internal benefit too. When prospects start telling reps "I've seen you all over my LinkedIn", sales teams quickly become marketing's biggest supporters.

## Steps

1. **Find one willing rep.** It only takes one adventurous account executive to pilot it.
2. **Set up a simple studio.** A local videographer can turn a meeting room into a professional-looking podcast set in a day.
3. **Write interview scripts from real pain points.** Use the problems and use cases prospects mention most often in calls.
4. **Record mini podcasts, then cut the best clips** into short ads, plus longer versions (up to ten minutes) that work as a demo of the value.
5. **Target tightly.** Run them to specific target account lists, at account and person level, matching each clip's pain point to that company type's use case.
6. **Optimise for watching, not leads.** Bid manually and well above LinkedIn's recommendation so the ads actually get delivered. Track audience penetration and frequency, not cost per lead. Five of the right people watching a ten-minute video is a win.
7. **Run the reps' own posts alongside** as thought leadership ads. Prospects often book demos straight from the direct messages.
8. **Hand high-watch accounts to outbound,** with the follow-up coming from the same rep who appears in the videos.

## Example

Jake Newby at Lendio films mini podcasts with Lendio's account executives and runs the best clips as video ads to target account lists. One $4,000 campaign booked five demos in two weeks, for deals worth $100,000 to $4.5 million in annual contract value. It outperforms all their other ad types by far. Lendio now also runs the videos to open deals, so champions have content to share internally and the rest of the buying committee has seen the rep before the call.

## Prerequisites

- At least one rep willing to be on camera.
- Target account lists with person-level targeting.
- A clear list of the pain points prospects raise in calls.

## How to measure

- Audience penetration and frequency within target accounts.
- Watch time on the longer videos, by account.
- Reply rate on outbound to high-watch accounts versus cold outbound.
- Demos and pipeline influenced, and how often prospects mention the videos on calls.

## Pitfalls

- Judging it on cost per lead and cutting it too early.
- Letting LinkedIn's suggested bids under-deliver to a small audience.
- Scripts written from marketing's view rather than what prospects actually say.
- A different person doing the follow-up, which wastes the familiarity the videos built.
