## Why it works

People trust people more than brands. An idea from a founder or a customer feels like an opinion worth considering. The same idea in a branded ad feels like marketing.

Removing the call to action matters as much as the messenger. The moment a reader recognises a sales attempt, they raise their defences and discount what they read. A post that asks for nothing does not trigger that filter, so the point of view actually gets absorbed.

Repeated over weeks, these posts make your company and its people familiar to the whole buying committee. When a need comes up, you are the vendor they already feel they know.

## Steps

1. **Pick the voices.** Your founder or CEO, two or three happy customers, and partners your buyers respect.
2. **Collect posts that teach or share a point of view.** Lessons, opinions and stories about the buyer's problem. Not product announcements.
3. **Promote them as thought leader ads** to your target account list, filtered to the buying committee job titles.
4. **Leave out the call to action.** No "book a demo", no link to the website. The goal is attention and trust.
5. **Rotate fresh posts in regularly** so the same accounts see a range of ideas rather than one post on repeat.
6. **Measure influence, not clicks.** Track which target accounts saw the ads and whether they later progress into pipeline.

## Example

Tabs spends 60% of its LinkedIn budget on thought leadership ads without calls to action. These ads send almost no traffic to the website, yet they influence most of the pipeline. Primer's data shows ABM advertisers put 51% of LinkedIn spend into upper-funnel formats like this.

## Prerequisites

- A target account list and buying committee titles.
- People willing to post regularly under their own names.
- A way to connect ad impressions to accounts and pipeline (for example Fibbler).

## How to measure

- Target accounts reached and engagement rate within them.
- Pipeline from accounts that saw the ads versus those that did not.
- Multi-touch or influence reports. Last-click will show nothing, by design.

## Pitfalls

- Judging it on last-click attribution and cutting it because "it drives no traffic".
- Promoting posts that are thinly disguised product pitches.
- Running it to a broad audience instead of named accounts, so the trust builds with the wrong people.
