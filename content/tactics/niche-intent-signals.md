## Why it works

Generic intent signals are shared by every competitor watching the same data, so prospects get the same messages from everyone. A niche signal is one only you care about, because it maps directly to your product's value. That means you reach people at the right moment, when no one else realises it is the right moment.

The message can also be about their exact situation. "We help companies deal with the complexity that comes with adding Snowflake" reads as if it was written for them, because it was. People pay much closer attention to messages about themselves.

And because nobody else is saying it, the message stands out.

## Steps

1. **Ask what changes inside a company just before it needs you.** Look at your best customers: what was happening to them when they bought?
2. **Turn that change into an observable signal.** Job postings, technology adoption, funding, regulation, or changes in their own marketing activity.
3. **Track it across your target accounts** using job and technographic data.
4. **Write messaging specific to that signal.** Name the situation, explain why it creates the problem, and say how you help.
5. **Run ads and outreach together** to the accounts showing the signal.
6. **Review what converts** and keep refining the signal.

## Example

Datadog's best customers have spikes in tech stack complexity. A niche signal might be a company hiring a Snowflake engineer, which suggests their stack is getting more complex. That supports ads and outreach about handling "Snowflake-induced tech stack complexity". Another example from the same article: a company with in-house auditors that serves customers needing several security frameworks could track job postings that mention multiple frameworks, then run ads about being uniquely placed to help.

## Prerequisites

- A clear understanding of why your best customers bought, and when.
- A way to track the signal, such as job posting or technographic data.

## How to measure

- Meeting and reply rates for signal-based outreach versus generic outreach.
- Conversion from signal to opportunity, to confirm the signal really predicts demand.

## Pitfalls

- Using signals every competitor also watches.
- Picking a signal that is interesting but does not predict buying.
- Mentioning the signal in a way that feels like surveillance. Talk about the situation, not about how you found out.
