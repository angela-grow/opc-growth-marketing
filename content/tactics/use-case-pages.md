## Why it works

Many buyers do not know your category exists. They only know the outcome they want: "scrape this site for me" or "automate my shopping". They will never search for your product name. These open searches are where new customers come from, and the only way to win them is to meet people at the problem they already have.

A page built around one specific task reads as if it was written for the person searching, because in effect it was. That match grabs attention and builds instant relevance.

When an assistant looks for an answer to that task, a page that answers it directly gives it something to latch onto and recommend.

## Steps

1. **List the tasks, not the features.** Write down every specific outcome someone might want that your product delivers.
2. **Build one page per task.** Each page answers one specific search: what the task is, how to do it, and how your product does it.
3. **Automate production, with a human approving.** An internal agent can draft a new page every day for someone to approve.
4. **Give the agent real context.** Early pages written without it are generic, and generic pages neither rank nor get recommended. Feed it your brand guidelines, product story, writing style and keyword research.
5. **Promote the winners.** Run the best-performing pages as ads.

## Example

Browserbase built an internal skill that drafts a new use-case page every day, which the team approves in Slack. The first pages were too generic, so they gave the skill their brand guidelines, platform story, blog posts as a style guide and a Semrush API key to do its own research. Their /use-case/agents page tripled its traffic in a month, and the best performers now run as ads.

## Prerequisites

- A clear list of the jobs your product does.
- Brand and style guidance good enough to keep automated pages from sounding generic.
- Someone to review each page before it goes live.

## How to measure

- Organic and AI-referred traffic per page.
- Sign-ups from use-case pages and time from visit to sign-up.
- Number of pages ranking for their target search.

## Pitfalls

- Thin, templated pages with only the keyword swapped.
- Writing for product-aware buyers who would have found you anyway.
- Publishing automatically without a human check.
