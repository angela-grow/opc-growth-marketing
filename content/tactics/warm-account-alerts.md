## Why it works

Most outreach fails on timing, not message. When an account starts clicking, reacting and visiting from your ads, the problem you solve is on their mind right now. Reaching out in that window means your message lands while it is relevant, instead of weeks later when the moment has passed.

The prospect also already knows your name. They have seen your ads several times, so a call or email feels like a continuation rather than an interruption from a stranger. Familiarity lowers the guard.

There is a second, internal piece of psychology here: sales teams ignore signals they do not trust. A short list of accounts, each with a plain reason ("142 LinkedIn engagements this month, up 212%"), is easy to act on. A long, unexplained list of "intent" gets ignored.

## Steps

1. **Pick where the signal goes.** Send it wherever sales already works: the CRM (a company record with the reason written on it), a Slack or Teams channel, or an enrichment tool like Clay.
2. **Choose the channels that feed it.** LinkedIn ad engagement, Google Ads clicks and Meta Ads clicks. Narrow to specific campaigns if some are pure awareness.
3. **Define "warm".** Do not send everyone who engaged. Send accounts that are new, or whose engagement is at least double last month's. Rising engagement is the signal, not the total.
4. **Filter by fit.** Set your ICP: industries, company sizes and countries you actually sell to. Anything outside it never reaches sales.
5. **Set cadence and a cap.** Daily or weekly, with a maximum per run (for example 50). Send each account once, and only resend if it heats up again or appears on a new channel.
6. **Run in preview for a week.** Look at who would have been sent before writing anything to the CRM. Adjust the filters until sales would agree with the list.
7. **Agree the follow-up play with sales.** Decide what a rep does with a warm account: research the buying committee, reference the topic of the ads, and reach out within a set number of days.

## Example

Tabs runs all their ads through Primer and uses Fibbler to identify which accounts have a spike in engagement on LinkedIn and Google Ads. Fibbler sends that data via webhook to Clay, where it feeds into their account scoring and tiering in HubSpot.

## Prerequisites

- Ads running to a defined target account list, so engagement can be tied to companies.
- A clear ICP definition sales agrees with.
- An agreed "what happens next" play for reps.

## How to measure

- Meetings booked and pipeline created from warm accounts versus other outbound.
- Reply rate on warm-account outreach versus cold outreach.
- Time from "warm" alert to first touch by sales.

## Pitfalls

- Sending every engaged account. Volume kills trust; sales stops looking.
- No reason attached. "This account is warm" without the why gets ignored.
- Opening with "I saw you clicked our ad". Use the signal to time and shape outreach, not as the opening line.
