## Why it works

Timing is the biggest factor in booking meetings. An average message to the right person at the right moment beats a perfect, well-researched message to someone with no need. Signals tell you when the moment is right, while the problem is top of mind.

Stopping at the account level loses sales. "Disney visited our website" leaves a rep asking who to contact, what to say and why. A short list of named people, each with a plain reason, removes that work. Reps can act on 15 to 20 clear names; a long list of "warm accounts" gets ignored, and once sales loses trust in marketing's lists it is hard to win back.

Running ads to the same people at the same time means that when the email or call arrives, the name is already familiar.

## Steps

1. **Optimise for meetings, not "warm accounts".** Agree with sales that meetings are the goal. Pipeline follows meetings.
2. **Score and tier your accounts** so you only track signals within your ideal customer profile.
3. **Map signals that actually show intent,** at both contact and account level. For example: product usage, job changes, hiring managers, review site intent, news alerts, ad and microsite engagement, and email engagement.
4. **Track them at scale** across all your ideal-customer accounts, and keep testing new signals.
5. **Weight the signals** by the business value and account value they represent.
6. **Compress everything into a weekly list** of 15 to 20 people per rep, each with a concise "reason to reach out now".
7. **Give reps a ready-made outreach cadence** that uses each person's account and behaviour context.
8. **Surround the same people with ads,** direct mail and account-level LinkedIn ads at the same time. Ad clicks become another signal for next week's list.

## Example

Datadog's Global Head of ABM and Campaigns, Kevin Driscoll, books around 25 meetings a week with Fortune 500 companies this way. Out of 150,000 accounts in their total market and 30,000 that fit their ideal customer profile, only around 112 are showing intent signals at any given time. One sprint helped an SDR team hit 75% of its monthly meeting quota in a single day. In his words: "The moment everything got easier was when I stopped optimizing for 'warm accounts' and started optimizing for meetings."

## Prerequisites

- A scored, tiered account universe in your CRM.
- Data infrastructure to collect and weight signals. Datadog has data engineers helping, but a simpler version can start in a spreadsheet.
- Sales agreement on how quickly lists get worked.

## How to measure

- Meetings booked per rep from the weekly list.
- Reply rate and meeting rate by signal type, to find the signals that matter.
- Share of list contacts reps actually worked.

## Pitfalls

- Sending accounts without people, or people without reasons.
- Adding more signals than you can weight sensibly.
- Lists too long for a rep to work in a week.
