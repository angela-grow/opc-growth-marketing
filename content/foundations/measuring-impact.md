Think about how a buyer actually finds a vendor. They read a newsletter and hear about you for the first time. They look up your CEO on LinkedIn and visit the website. Two weeks later a retargeting ad brings them to a webinar. A month later a rep emails, and they reply "Yes, we were actually evaluating you."

Last-click attribution gives that whole deal to the sales email and ignores everything that made it possible. Over-steer on last click and your budget drifts into search ads while cost per lead climbs.

## Three lenses

1. **Attribution.** Which channel gets credit for the conversion? Use all traceable touches to see where paid showed up in the journey.
2. **Influence.** Was a channel part of the account's journey, even without direct credit? This matters most in B2B, where the person who sees an ad is often not the person who converts.
3. **Incrementality.** Would the outcome have happened without the activity? This is the strongest evidence, and it needs a holdout group that does not see the ads.

## What the data shows

Primer's research across 379 B2B advertisers found:

- **Website analytics understate LinkedIn.** Many LinkedIn leads come through native lead forms and never reach the website.
- **The model changes the winner.** Switching from last touch to any touch grows Google's credit 1.1x, Meta's 1.6x and LinkedIn's 7x.
- **Platforms over-report.** Every platform reported more conversions than could be independently confirmed.

## Practical advice

- Keep a simple last-touch report for leadership, but do not use it alone to judge awareness tactics.
- For long sales cycles, watch **website visit lift** from exposed accounts rather than waiting for form fills. Where both could be measured, visit lift and form-fill lift moved in the same direction 92% of the time.
- Look for **triangulation**: where several different measures agree that a channel is working.
- Set up measurement **before** you launch, or you will not be able to prove anything afterwards.
